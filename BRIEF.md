# Project brief: StudyAssistant

## The competition
- I’m entering the Presidential SI Challenge (Standard Track), a national K–12 competition. My age group: Middle school (grades 6–8)
- Judges score only a written entry (a PDF of up to 10 pages) and a video of up to 4 minutes. They reward a clear understanding of the problem and a sensible use of AI.
- My deadlines: You can't miss December 18 or December 23 because on December 18 you need to be registered and the project is due on December 23.

## The problem
- People in my school struggle with learning because they think learning is a waste of time.
- Impact area: School and learning
- Why it matters (impact): The will be more excited about learning and be more encouraged to learn new things.
- Evidence it’s real: About 28% of U.S. students were chronically absent during the 2022–23 school year. “Chronically absent” usually means missing at least 10% of school days, or about 18 days. https://www.ed.gov/teaching-and-administration/supporting-students/chronic-absenteeism

- What people told me: Person A: Learning is honestly very boring and I get distracted all the time by other things and other students.

Person B: I miss school for more than 10 days every year. Sometimes I don't go because I am just bored of learning.

## My solution

- My AI’s job: My AI helps by generating a worksheet or a response to a essay, which works better than normal worksheets because you get a reward like playing a game or earning rewards and engages the user.

- Tools that already exist, and how mine is different: Tools like Wayground and Khan Academy already exist but they only use assigned worksheets and questions. Mine's will have AI made worksheets that include what the user needs.

- Safety plan: Risk: Students might type their names, grades, location, or essay drafts. Plan: Warning signs about not including personal information. Risk: The AI writes the whole essay and encourages cheating. Plan: ake the AI give hints, outlines, questions, and examples.

## My AI’s instructions

- When it answers: The user sends in work and says proofread or the user sends in questions and says make a worksheet.

- Its job: To proofread work, give tips, and make custom worksheets.

- Who it talks to and how to sound: The AI is talking to students who need help studying and the AI should talk in kind understandable sentences.

- Facts it must use: The AI uses the work that the students send in and nothing else.

- Rules it must follow: Never randomly fill out worksheets and help students too much and do all of their work for them. Don't randomly grade work.

- How to answer: The AI should send the graded work with notes that help the user understand the problem better. Send a worksheet that fits the users work level from very easy ones to hard ones.
